A decision, not a redesign

The bottom bar

You asked for it to go. It was built deliberately the day before you asked, because DESIGN SYSTEM.md §5.7 has specified it since the document was written — and the same paragraph ends “Never: hamburger menu hiding primary navigation.” Replacing the bar with a menu is a change to the specification, not a change of styling. Both options are drawn below; this one is yours.

Option A — what ships today

Keep the bar, sharpen it

Mbele Sign in
Board Search Categories Countries Tracker
  • + Five destinations always visible. A first-time visitor never has to discover a menu.
  • + It already hides on scroll down and returns on scroll up, so reading is full-height.
  • + One <nav> landmark, rendered once, laid out two ways. axe-clean.
  • − 56px is 6.6% of an iPhone 14 viewport whenever it is showing.
  • − It does read as an app rather than a web page — your objection, and it is fair.

Costs nothing to choose. The bar exists. This option is the rest of the redesign landing around it.

Option B — what you asked for

Bar out, sheet in

Mbele
  • + Full viewport height, always. One more opportunity row on every screen.
  • + Reads as a web page. The menu holds far more than five things — trust pages, messages, account, low-data.
  • − Contradicts §5.7 in writing. The spec needs amending, or the code stops matching it.
  • − Primary destinations go behind a tap. §5.7's reason for banning that is first-time discovery, and this product's readers mostly arrive once, from a link.

Costs a spec change. §5.7's “never” is edited, the reason recorded, and PrimaryNav.astro is rebuilt. The drawer itself is drawn on the Navigation artboard.

There is a third reading, and it may be the right one.

Your objection was that the bar “consumes too much vertical space”. It already hides on scroll — so the complaint may really be about the first screen, where the bar, the masthead and the header were all competing before the board appeared. The 270px masthead fixes that on its own, with the bar left alone. Worth looking at Option A on a real phone before spending a spec change on Option B.
